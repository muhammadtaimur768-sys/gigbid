# Programme Impact Report

A nonprofit client came to me because funders asked for impact numbers the charity could not produce.
When the scope changed halfway through, we re-planned in an afternoon rather than pretending it had not. Most of the hard problems were about messy inputs rather than clever algorithms. Demos happened every Friday, which kept feedback small and frequent.

## What I did

I used tableau to publish the operations workbook. Finance received a workbook with lookups, pivot tables and a protected assumptions tab they could edit themselves. I used Likert analysis to analyse the customer survey.

Duplicate customers were merged, broken dates repaired and free-text country names standardised. The nonprofit team had strong opinions about edge cases, and most of them turned out to be right. I wrote the documentation as I went instead of at the end, which made the handover painless.

## Results

The headline number: it secured 7 renewed grants. A pilot with one team came first; the wider rollout followed after two weeks of fixes. I shared a short written plan in the first week so nobody was surprised by scope later. We started with a two-day discovery session to agree on what success would look like and how it would be measured. We tracked a small set of numbers weekly and dropped anything nobody looked at. The client's own staff can now extend it without me, which was an explicit goal. We deliberately left out two features the client asked for at the start because they did not move the main metric. Data access took longer than the build itself; getting the right permissions was a project of its own. The first version was deliberately boring, and that is why it shipped on time. Handover included a recorded walkthrough and a short runbook for whoever is on call. Security review asked for audit logs on every action, so we added them early.

## Stack

Tableau, survey analysis, plus the usual tooling.
